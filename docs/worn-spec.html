01 — Core Insight

The dopamine was never in the delivery.

Online shopping's pleasure is in the anticipation loop — browsing, imagining yourself in it, building the cart, checking out, waiting for the tracker. The package arriving is frequently a letdown. Returns are proof: reality disappoints the fantasy.

Every existing solution tries to improve the reality (better try-on, accurate sizing, faster delivery). WORN does the opposite. It perfects the fantasy and strips out the reality entirely.

"The order flow is the product. The delivery notification is the product. The reveal is the reward."

02 — Product

Six surfaces, one loop.

WORN is built around a single repeating cycle: see → desire → commit → wait → reveal. Every surface serves that loop. Nothing exists that doesn't deepen the arc.

Surface 01

Avatar Onboarding

Upload 2–3 photos. The app builds your persistent fashion avatar — body shape, skin tone, hair, usual poses. This is your model profile. Every item you browse renders on you, not a stock body.

Surface 02

The Feed

A TikTok-paced vertical scroll of listings — from indie sellers, brands, and UGC creators. Every item auto-renders a try-on preview using your avatar. You're the model in every listing you see.

Surface 03

The Cart & Checkout

Feels exactly like real ecomm. Pick size, color, "delivery" speed. Express (2 hrs), Standard (overnight), Slow Burn (48 hrs). Pay with WORN Coins. The deliberate wait is engineered into the UX.

Surface 04

Order Tracking

Branded tracking page. "Processing → Packed → Out for delivery → Arriving today." Status updates push to notification. The arc takes real time. Instant would kill the dopamine — the wait is the design.

Surface 05

The Delivery Reveal

Notification: "Your haul arrived." Opens to a cinematic lookbook — AI renders of you in the item across 6+ scenarios: studio, street, golden hour, editorial, night. 2 free. Rest paywalled. The unboxing is photos.

Surface 06

The Lookbook

Your profile is a growing editorial archive of every "haul." Shareable. Social. Becomes a visual identity over time. Sellers can request to feature your renders in their listing. You earn coins.

The Coin Economy

WORN runs on an in-app currency that keeps the commerce feeling real without real money changing hands per-item. Coins are earned through engagement and purchased in bundles.

Earn

Coin Faucets

Daily login streak, sharing a lookbook post, opting your renders into a seller's listing, completing your avatar profile, inviting a friend, reviewing a "haul."

Spend

Coin Sinks

"Buying" items from the feed. Unlocking paywalled lookbook photos. Rushing an order to Express. Purchasing premium editorial render packs (cinematic, film grain, Parisian, etc.).

04 — Monetization

Three revenue streams. None rely on selling clothes.

IAP
Coin Bundles

Users buy coin packs to "shop" more, unlock lookbook photos, rush orders, or buy premium render styles. Freemium entry, IAP for depth. Primary user-side revenue.

B2B
Seller Listings

Brands and indie sellers pay to list. Free tier (limited impressions), paid plans for feed boost, analytics, and access to aggregate style data. Secondary but high-margin.

Aff.
Affiliate Bridge

"Buy the real thing" CTA on every deliver reveal. Affiliate redirect to the actual product. Commission on real purchases without WORN handling logistics. Zero ops overhead.

Unit Economics Logic

The core bet: a user who spends ₹200 on coins to "buy" a ₹4,000 outfit and unlock their lookbook photos is a user who might have never bought the outfit at all. The spend-to-value ratio is radically better than real fashion ecomm — no returns, no inventory, no logistics margin erosion.

The seller side is equally clean: a brand pays ₹500/month for premium listing access, gets AI-rendered product photos on real bodies at scale, and a community of potential buyers who are already emotionally invested in wearing their product. Cost-per-impression collapses.

"WORN clips both sides — users pay for the fantasy, brands pay for the distribution. The clothes never move and the margins never erode."

05 — Psychology

Why the wait is non-negotiable.

The temptation in product development is to make everything instant. Do not. The anticipation interval is the emotional core of WORN's dopamine mechanic. Here is why each timing choice is made deliberately:

Impulse kill

No instant delivery

Even "Express" is 2 hours. This mirrors real same-day delivery psychologically — fast but not free. The user's anticipation has to cook. Instant would flatten the reveal moment entirely.

Commitment device

Coins, not free

Even though coins cost less than real clothes, the spending act triggers loss aversion. Paid items feel more valuable. The reveal hits harder when something was given up for it.

Variable reward

Render quality varies

Not every render is equally good — lighting, pose, garment complexity all vary. Some lookbook drops are stunning, some are serviceable. Unpredictability deepens the pull-to-open behavior.

Social amplifier

Lookbook is shareable identity

The renders aren't just keepsakes — they're the best photos most users will have of themselves in fashion-forward contexts. They will share them. Every share is a cold acquisition ad.

The Order State Machine

The "delivery" timing is implemented as a Redis-backed state machine with BullMQ delayed jobs. On checkout, an order is created in state PROCESSING. BullMQ schedules state transitions at real intervals — the durations are tunable per delivery tier. Each transition fires a push notification and updates the tracking page in real time via polling or WebSocket.

States

Order States

PENDING → PROCESSING → PACKED → OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → ARRIVING_SOON → DELIVERED → REVEAL_READY

Timings (Express)

2-Hour Arc

PROCESSING: 5 min. PACKED: 20 min. OUT_FOR_DELIVERY: 60 min. ARRIVING_SOON: 90 min. DELIVERED: 120 min. Then render job triggers immediately.

Render Pipeline Logic

When an order hits DELIVERED, BullMQ fires a render job per item in the order. Each job hits the FASHN.ai (or self-hosted ComfyUI) endpoint with the user's stored avatar embeddings and the item's product images. The job generates 6–8 render variants across preset "scenarios" (studio, street, editorial, etc.). Results are stored in R2, metadata written to Postgres. When all renders complete, order state transitions to REVEAL_READY and a push notification fires.

07 — Risks & Mitigations

What could go wrong.

Render quality

High

If AI try-on renders look uncanny or inaccurate, the fantasy collapses. The product only works if users feel genuinely flattered by their lookbook photos.

→ Use FASHN.ai for feed previews (fast, good enough), reserve higher-quality multi-step pipeline (ComfyUI + upscaler) for the Reveal renders. Quality bar for Reveal must be high — this is the moment that sells the app.

Perception: "fake shopping"

Medium

Users might feel deceived when they realize nothing ships. The "fake" framing could damage trust or feel cynical.

→ Be transparent from day one. WORN's premise is explicitly "the shopping experience, not the shopping outcome." Frame it as entertainment and self-expression, not a substitute for real retail. The onboarding states this clearly and proudly.

Avatar photo privacy

High

Users uploading photos of themselves for AI rendering is a significant trust and legal surface. Misuse (deepfakes, data brokers) would be catastrophic for the brand.

→ On-device embedding extraction where possible. No raw photo storage beyond initial processing. Explicit, granular consent for render opt-in to seller listings. GDPR/DPDP compliant deletion flow. Clearly stated: your renders are yours.

Coin economy balance

Medium

If earning coins is too easy, nobody buys IAP. If it's too hard, users churn before experiencing the reveal.

→ New users get a generous coin grant in onboarding (enough for 2-3 orders). Earning is front-loaded early, then tapers to push toward IAP. A/B test the earn/spend curve aggressively in the first 3 months.

Render cost at scale

Medium

AI rendering APIs cost money per generation. At scale, render cost per order could exceed coin revenue per order.

→ Feed previews use cheap fast-tier API. Full reveal renders are batched off-peak using BullMQ. Self-hosted ComfyUI on GPU box is the endgame once volume justifies it. Pricing model calibrated so paywall unlock covers render cost.

09 — Open Questions

What we don't know yet.

Product

Optimal wait duration

Is 2 hours the right floor for Express? Does longer anticipation increase reveal satisfaction, or does it increase drop-off? Needs to be A/B tested in beta.

Product

Paywall split

2 free / 6 paywalled is the starting hypothesis. Do more free renders reduce IAP? Or do they increase it by setting quality expectations? Unclear without data.

Growth

Primary acquisition channel

Shared lookbook renders are the organic hypothesis. But is the demographic that shares fashion content also the demographic that enjoys the "game" layer? May need to separate.

Legal

Avatar likeness rights

When a user's renders appear in a seller's listing, is there a licensing model needed? Does it vary by jurisdiction? Requires legal review before seller marketplace launch.
